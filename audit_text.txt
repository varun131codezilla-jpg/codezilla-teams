CODEZILLA TEAMS
Technical SEO & GEO Audit
codezilla-teams.vercel.app  |  Prepared for Haider  |  July 28, 2026
1. Executive Summary
Codezilla Teams is a lead-generation site for a B2B professional service (India Employer of Record / Contractor of Record). Revenue depends entirely on organic and AI-assisted discovery converting into consultation bookings — there is no product to try, no marketplace, nothing but content and trust signals driving a form fill. That makes SEO and GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) the highest-leverage growth channel available outside paid ads and outbound.
The content strategy is strong: transparent pricing, named founders, a public compliance playbook, named client logos, and an FAQ section are exactly the trust signals both Google and AI answer engines reward. But the technical foundation is not built out — the site is currently invisible to structured-data consumers and has real crawlability gaps that cap how far the content strategy can travel.
Bottom line: content quality is 8/10. Technical SEO execution is 3/10. Closing that gap is mostly quick, low-cost engineering work — most fixes below are under a day of dev time each.
2. What This Site Actually Sells (for context)
Codezilla Teams is an Employer of Record (EOR) and Contractor of Record (COR) service that lets foreign companies (US/UK/EU) legally hire employees in India without setting up an Indian subsidiary. Codezilla Global Technologies Pvt. Ltd. becomes the legal employer, handling payroll, TDS, PF/ESIC, statutory compliance, and labor liability, while the client company retains day-to-day management of the employee. Positioning is against generic IT staffing/outsourcing agencies, with three deliberate wedges:
Radical pricing transparency — flat fees ($199–$299/employee/month EOR, $49/month contractor), published openly with no "talk to sales" gate.
Zero lock-in — a $0 transition fee if the client later incorporates their own Indian entity and moves the team over.
India-only specialization from an Indore HQ, positioned against platforms that support 150 countries with none of them deeply.
This is a considered B2B purchase (legal/compliance risk, recurring spend) researched by CTOs, People Ops leads, and founders — exactly the audience now starting research inside ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews before ever visiting a website. That is why GEO is treated as a first-class channel in this report, not an afterthought.
3. Site Inventory
Static-rendered pages crawled: Home (/), About (/about), Services (/services), Pricing (/pricing), How It Works (/how-it-works), Industries (/industries), Compliance Playbook (/compliance-playbook), Contact (/contact). Hosted on Vercel. Content is server-rendered HTML (good for crawlers — not a JS-only SPA), single H1 per page, descriptive title tags, and alt text present on all sampled images.

Internal linking vs. canonical path
WARNING
Every internal link (nav, buttons, footer) points to the .html filename — e.g. href="/pricing.html" — but the server 301/308-redirects that request to the clean path /pricing.
Every single internal click costs an unnecessary redirect hop. At small scale this is a minor speed tax, but it also means your internal link equity is being passed through a redirect instead of directly, and it’s inconsistent with what you actually want indexed. 4.3 Canonicalization
Area
Status
Finding
Why It Matters / Fix
Canonical tags
CRITICAL
Zero pages (checked: home, pricing, and consistent with the shared template used across the site) include a <link rel="canonical"> tag.
Without canonicals, you’re relying entirely on redirects to prevent duplicate-content splitting between the .html and clean URLs, and you have no defense against parameter variants (e.g. ?utm_source=...) being indexed as separate pages. Add a self-referencing canonical to every page — this is a 1-line template change, highest ROI item in this report.

5. GEO — Generative Engine Optimization
GEO is the practice of making a site legible and citable to AI answer engines (ChatGPT/GPTBot, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews, Claude). For a considered B2B purchase like EOR services, buyers increasingly ask an AI "who are the best India EOR providers" or "what does an India EOR cost" before visiting any site directly — if your page isn’t structured for extraction, you’re invisible in that conversation even if your content is excellent.
5.1 What This Site Already Does Right for GEO
Server-rendered HTML — AI crawlers (GPTBot, PerplexityBot, ClaudeBot, Google-Extended) largely do not execute JavaScript. Because content is present in raw HTML, it is actually readable by them today.
Specific, quotable facts — exact pricing ($199–$299/employee/month), exact timelines (3 business days), exact certifications (ISO 27001:2022, SOC 2 Type II) are the kind of concrete, citable statements LLMs prefer to quote over vague marketing copy.
A public, detailed compliance playbook — long-form, specific, technical content like this is exactly what gets pulled into AI answers about "how does India EOR handle termination" or "India EOR statutory compliance" because it out-specifies generic competitor content.
Named, identifiable entities — founders with names and titles, a registered CIN, a physical address. LLMs weight entity clarity heavily when deciding whether to trust and cite a source.
5.2 GEO Gaps to Close
Area
Status
Finding
Why It Matters / Fix
Structured data
CRITICAL
Same root cause as the SEO gap in 4.4 — no Organization, Service, or FAQPage schema.
Schema is one of the clearest signals AI engines use to extract structured facts (price, address, FAQ answers) with confidence instead of having to parse prose. This is the single highest-leverage GEO fix available — see Section 6.
FAQPage markup on FAQ content
CRITICAL
The pricing page has a genuine, well-written FAQ section but it is plain HTML, not marked up as FAQPage schema.
FAQ schema is directly consumed by both Google’s AI Overviews and several AI assistants as a source of pre-formatted Q&A pairs. Mark this up and expand FAQ content to Services and Industries pages.
llms.txt
WARNING
No /llms.txt file (an emerging, not-yet-universal convention some AI crawlers check for a curated summary of a site).
Low cost, asymmetric-upside addition: a short llms.txt at the root summarizing what Codezilla Teams does, key facts (pricing, location, certifications), and links to key pages. Not yet guaranteed to be read by major crawlers, but costs under an hour and several competitors in the AI-tooling space already ship one.
Third-party corroboration
WARNING
No evidence of listings on G2, Clutch, Capterra, or a Wikipedia/Crunchbase-style profile — LLMs weight independent corroboration heavily, not just on-site claims.
Claim a Clutch and G2 profile (standard for B2B services) and get 3–5 client reviews posted there. AI engines cross-reference these when asked "best EOR provider in India" comparison-style questions — right now there is nothing off-site to be found and summarized.
Author/entity signals (About page)
WARNING
Founder bios exist but aren’t marked up as Person schema linked to the Organization, and LinkedIn is the only sameAs signal available (X and Instagram exist on the Contact page but aren’t consistently cross-linked from About).
Add Person schema for both founders with sameAs links to all their public profiles, and an Organization schema with sameAs covering LinkedIn, X, and Instagram. This builds the "knowledge graph" clarity that both Google’s Knowledge Panel and AI entity resolution rely on.
Comparison-ready content
PASS (partial)
The "IT Agency vs. Codezilla Teams" comparison table on the homepage is exactly the format AI engines like to lift when answering "how is X different from Y" — but it’s the only comparison asset on the site.
Build 1–2 more explicit comparison pages/sections: "Codezilla Teams vs. Deel/Remote/Multiplier" (named-competitor comparison, if legally comfortable) or "EOR vs. setting up your own India entity" as a dedicated page. Named comparisons are disproportionately surfaced when someone asks an AI to compare vendors.
